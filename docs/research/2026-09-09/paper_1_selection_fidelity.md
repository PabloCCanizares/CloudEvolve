# Paper 1 — Selection Fidelity and Trajectory Consequences

**Status:** GO — highest priority  
**Type:** phenomenon / characterization + evaluation methodology  
**Working title:** *From Prediction Fidelity to Selection Fidelity: How Surrogate Errors Alter Multiobjective Evolutionary Search*

Alternative title: *Selection Fidelity in Surrogate-Assisted NSGA-II: From Prediction Errors to Trajectory Damage*.

## 1. Scientific thesis

The paper studies surrogate fidelity at the level of the **actual decisions made by the multiobjective optimizer**.

The central hierarchy is:

\[
\text{prediction fidelity}
\neq
\text{ranking fidelity}
\neq
\text{dominance fidelity}
\neq
\text{selection fidelity}
\neq
\text{trajectory fidelity}.
\]

Previous CloudEvolve work already shows that global predictive accuracy can remain favorable while regional ranking fidelity deteriorates during optimization. That result is useful motivation, but it is not enough for an independent paper: recent work on offline model-based optimization, learning to rank, local surrogate validation and relative/pairwise surrogate models already makes a pure `prediction error vs ranking` story too incremental.

The new scientific question is therefore:

> **Which surrogate errors actually change NSGA-II environmental-selection decisions, and which of those decision changes cause persistent downstream search degradation?**

## 2. Why ranking fidelity is not enough

A surrogate can preserve the marginal order of each objective and even preserve pairwise Pareto dominance while changing NSGA-II's final survivor set.

For NSGA-II, survival depends on:

1. non-dominated sorting;
2. front assignment;
3. crowding distance inside the partially accepted front;
4. truncation of that front.

For objective \(m\), crowding uses differences between neighboring objective values:

\[
CD_i = \sum_m
\frac{f_m(i+1)-f_m(i-1)}{f_m^{max}-f_m^{min}}.
\]

A strictly monotone nonlinear transformation can preserve Spearman and dominance but alter normalized neighbor distances. Therefore:

\[
\rho_S=1
\not\Rightarrow
\text{same crowding order}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{same survivors}.
\]

A constructed illustrative counterexample already demonstrates the logical possibility: Spearman is 1 and dominance is preserved, yet NSGA-II selects a different set because of crowding, with real hypervolume changing from 70 to 48. This is a conceptual demonstration, **not CloudEvolve experimental evidence**.

## 3. Main research questions

### RQ1 — From predictive fidelity to selection fidelity

**To what extent do conventional predictive, ranking and dominance metrics predict the fidelity of NSGA-II environmental-selection decisions?**

### RQ2 — Ordinally perfect but decisionally different

**Can surrogate objective representations preserve very high or perfect ordinal/dominance fidelity while inducing different NSGA-II survivor sets in real optimization checkpoints?**

### RQ3 — Immediate consequence

**Which surrogate-induced selection errors produce significant one-step selection regret?**

### RQ4 — Trajectory consequence

**Which selection errors propagate into persistent downstream trajectory degradation, and which are absorbed by evolutionary search?**

### RQ5 — Feedback / optimizer-induced shift

**How does the distributional shift generated by the optimizer affect selection fidelity throughout the search?**

## 4. Core metrics

### Prediction fidelity

- MAE;
- RMSE;
- MAPE;
- \(R^2\).

### Ranking fidelity

- Spearman;
- Kendall;
- pairwise ordering accuracy.

### Dominance fidelity

Measure agreement between predicted and real pairwise dominance relations.

### Front-assignment fidelity

If \(r_i\) is the true non-dominated rank and \(\hat r_i\) the predicted rank:

\[
A_{front}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i \mathbf 1[r_i=\hat r_i].
\]

### Crowding fidelity

Compare true and surrogate crowding order inside each front, e.g. by Spearman/Kendall over crowding scores.

### Selection fidelity

Let \(S_F(C_t)\) be the survivor set using simulator objectives and \(S_{\hat F}(C_t)\) the survivor set using surrogate objectives.

Selection overlap:

\[
A_{sel}=\frac{|S_F\cap S_{\hat F}|}{\mu}.
\]

Jaccard fidelity:

\[
J_{sel}=\frac{|S_F\cap S_{\hat F}|}{|S_F\cup S_{\hat F}|}.
\]

False elimination rate:

\[
FER=\frac{|S_F\setminus S_{\hat F}|}{|S_F|}.
\]

### One-step selection regret

Evaluate both selected sets using **real simulator objectives**:

\[
R_{sel}=HV(F(S_F))-HV(F(S_{\hat F})).
\]

This distinguishes harmless disagreement from costly disagreement.

## 5. Experiment A — One-step counterfactual selection

For selected real optimization checkpoints:

1. reconstruct the candidate pool \(C_t\);
2. recover the surrogate predictions available at that checkpoint;
3. obtain real simulator ground truth for the complete pool or a predeclared auditable subset;
4. run NSGA-II environmental selection with real objectives;
5. run the same selection with surrogate objectives;
6. compare fronts, crowding, survivors and real one-step regret.

The main analysis is not simply `metric vs HV`. It is:

\[
\text{surrogate metric}
\rightarrow
\text{selection disagreement}
\rightarrow
R_{sel}.
\]

A particularly valuable empirical result would be repeated checkpoints with:

\[
Spearman\approx1,\quad A_{dom}\approx1,
\]

but:

\[
A_{sel}<1\quad\text{and}\quad R_{sel}>0.
\]

## 6. Experiment B — Branching trajectory intervention

One-step errors do not necessarily matter long term. To measure downstream consequences, branch the optimization from the same checkpoint:

- **Branch R:** environmental selection using real objectives;
- **Branch S:** environmental selection using surrogate objectives.

Continue both branches for a predeclared horizon \(h\) using matched settings, seeds/common random numbers where feasible, operators, population size, budgets and retraining policy.

Primary trajectory endpoint:

\[
\Delta HV(h)=HV_R(t+h)-HV_S(t+h).
\]

Additional endpoints:

- archive overlap;
- population overlap;
- loss/recovery of extreme solutions;
- Pareto-region coverage;
- recovery time;
- Calls@HV.

This separates:

\[
\text{decision disagreement}
\]

from:

\[
\text{harmful decision disagreement}.
\]

## 7. Distribution-shift analysis

Use feedback/optimizer-induced covariate shift as a conceptual foundation:

\[
D_t\rightarrow M_t\rightarrow optimizer\rightarrow Q_{t+1}\rightarrow D_{t+1}.
\]

The paper should test whether movement away from the original training/query regime is associated with loss of selection fidelity, rather than merely with higher pointwise error.

Do not claim that optimizer-induced shift itself is novel.

## 8. External validation

Use **Off-MOO-Bench** as an external validation source where feasible.

The first external experiment does not need to reproduce the entire CloudEvolve online loop. Candidate pools with known objective values can be used to study:

\[
prediction\rightarrow ranking\rightarrow dominance\rightarrow selection.
\]

External validation is particularly valuable if targeting IEEE TEVC.

## 9. Relation to the current CloudEvolve/XAI manuscript

The current manuscript already contains:

- global vs regional fidelity;
- predictive/ranking degradation during optimization;
- surrogate-assisted savings;
- structural GraphSAGE observations;
- XAI-v1 acquisition and ratification.

This paper is independent only if its central evidence is genuinely new:

\[
\boxed{
\text{environmental-selection fidelity}
+
\text{counterfactual checkpoint interventions}
+
\text{trajectory consequences}
}
\]

Repeating the existing MAPE/Spearman tables with more seeds is **not sufficient**.

## 10. Minimum experiment

- validated CloudEvolve checkpoints;
- real truth for candidate pools;
- predictive/ranking/dominance/front/crowding metrics;
- selection fidelity;
- one-step selection regret;
- multiple seeds/checkpoints.

## 11. Strong experiment

- multiple surrogate families;
- multiple workload regimes;
- longitudinal checkpoint analysis;
- branching trajectories;
- Off-MOO-Bench validation;
- blocked/hierarchical statistical analysis respecting repeated observations within seeds/problems.

## 12. Novelty boundary

Do **not** claim novelty for:

- ranking-aware surrogate modeling;
- optimizer-induced OOD;
- local surrogate validation;
- pairwise surrogate models;
- model selection based on optimization relevance;
- learning environmental selection itself.

The intended gap is:

> **directly measuring whether approximate surrogate objectives preserve a MOEA's environmental-selection decisions and quantifying the downstream consequences of the decisions they change.**

## 13. Go / merge / drop

### GO

If:

- selection fidelity contains meaningful information beyond ranking/dominance;
- selection errors are reproducible across checkpoints/seeds;
- some errors have measurable real regret;
- branching experiments show persistent downstream effects or clearly characterize when errors are harmless.

### REDUCE / MERGE

If selection fidelity is almost completely explained by existing ranking/dominance metrics or trajectory damage is negligible.

### DROP as an independent paper

If surrogate and real environmental selections are almost always functionally equivalent.
